Ideas for goals 2026

Each Champion must contribute one custom rule to the company’s static analysis (SAST) or linting tools to prevent a recurring bug found in their specific tech stack.

The Champion must mentor at least one non-champion peer to perform a security code review. Success is measured by the peer identifying a security flaw without the Champion's intervention. (only relevant for some). How do we approach this? What is my role in this?

Security goals included in the Developer’s performance review.


2025 Technical Review: A factual review of the security improvements you led and the training you completed.

Friction Analysis: Identifying specific blockers (technical or departmental) that prevented you from executing security tasks in 2025.

2026 Goal Setting: Transitioning your goals from "activity-based" (attending meetings) to "outcome-based" (vulnerability triage, security debt reduction, and automated guardrails).

Capacity Audit: Confirming that your 10% time allocation remains viable given your team's current roadmap.

Please come prepared to list the top three security risks currently residing in your team's codebase or workflow that you intend to prioritize in the coming quarters.


"When you identify a security improvement, does the Team Lead prioritize it in the sprint, or does it move to the bottom of the backlog? Provide a specific example."

Tooling Efficacy: "Which of our current security tools (SAST/DAST/SCA) provides the most 'noise' or false positives for your team? Why haven't we silenced those alerts yet?"

Dependency Risk: "How long does it currently take your team to update a vulnerable library once a Dependabot (or equivalent) alert is raised?"


Level	Profile	2026 Expectation
Level 1: Passive	Attends meetings, does training, but doesn't change team behavior.	Action: Move to Level 2 or exit the program.
Level 2: Reactive	Fixes vulnerabilities when told, participates in security discussions.	Action: Empower to lead one automated guardrail project.
Level 3: Proactive	Threat models new features, blocks insecure PRs, mentors others.	Action: Give them "Security Lead" designation and budget.

It's been a year since you started as a security champion. I'd like to check in on how things are going and how the experience has been for you so far.

Here are 3 questions in advance you can already think about to prepare the meeting:
 - How has your first year as a security champion been so far? Did it meet your expectations? Do you feel well equipped for the role or do you need more support? What can we do to improve?
 - Did you actually spend 10% of your time (4 hours a week) on security? If not, what specific feature or deadline overrode this commitment?
 - Which security improvements do you see as the top priorities for 2026?
